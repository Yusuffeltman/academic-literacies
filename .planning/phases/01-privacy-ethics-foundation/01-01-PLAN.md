**File 2: governance/dpia/02-lawful-basis.md**

For EACH data collection activity listed in 01-processing-description.md, map it against POPIA's 8 conditions for lawful processing using the structure from the research document (Pattern 1):

1. **Accountability (Section 8)** -- responsible party, information officer, delegated processing
2. **Processing Limitation (Sections 9-12)** -- lawful basis is legitimate interest (Section 11(1)(f)) PLUS contractual necessity (Section 11(1)(b)). NOT consent-only. Document the legitimate interest assessment for each activity.
3. **Purpose Specification (Sections 13-14)** -- specific pedagogical purpose, retention limit (end of academic year), how students are notified
4. **Further Processing Limitation (Section 15)** -- permitted secondary uses (aggregated cohort analysis for teaching team), explicitly prohibited uses (punitive action, surveillance, third-party commercial use)
5. **Information Quality (Section 16)** -- accuracy measures, handling missing data from connectivity gaps
6. **Openness (Sections 17-18)** -- privacy notice requirements, plain language description
7. **Security Safeguards (Sections 19-22)** -- reference to safeguards document (04-safeguards.md)
8. **Data Subject Participation (Sections 23-25)** -- reference to rights document (05-data-subject-rights.md)

For conditions 7 and 8, include a brief summary and cross-reference to the dedicated documents (04 and 05) rather than duplicating full content.

Reference the USAf POPIA Code of Conduct for university-specific guidance. Reference the Werksmans legitimate interest analysis approach. Avoid the "consent myth" pitfall documented in research.

**File 3: governance/dpia/03-risk-assessment.md**

Create a privacy risk assessment covering:

1. **Risk identification** -- for each data collection activity, identify privacy risks. Include at minimum:
   - Re-identification risk from aggregated data (small cohort sizes at teaching team level)
   - Data breach risk (unauthorized access to individual student data)
   - Function creep risk (data used beyond pedagogical purpose)
   - Connectivity-gap data quality risk (incomplete data leading to inaccurate student profiles)
   - Tutor access boundary risk (tutor accessing non-assigned student data)
   - Retention overrun risk (data kept beyond academic year)

2. **Impact assessment** -- for each risk, rate:
   - Likelihood (Low/Medium/High)
   - Impact on data subject (Low/Medium/High)
   - Overall risk level (Low/Medium/High/Critical)

3. **Risk-specific notes** -- the re-identification risk is explicitly called out in the research (Pitfall 3): define minimum group size of 10 for aggregation at teaching team level. Document rationale (research recommendation).

**File 4: governance/dpia/04-safeguards.md**

For each identified risk in 03-risk-assessment.md, document mitigation measures:

1. **Technical safeguards:**
   - Encryption at rest and in transit
   - Role-based access control enforcement (student/tutor/teaching team/admin)
   - Tutor-student mapping enforcement (tutor sees only assigned students)
   - Minimum group size enforcement for aggregated views (suppress cells below threshold of 10)
   - Audit logging of data access
   - Automated deletion at end of academic year

2. **Organizational safeguards:**
   - Staff training on data handling
   - Tutor onboarding covering data access responsibilities
   - Clear data handling policy communicated to students
   - Incident response procedure for data breaches
   - Regular review cycle (annual DPIA refresh)

3. **Residual risk** -- after safeguards, what risk remains and is it acceptable?

**File 5: governance/dpia/05-data-subject-rights.md**

Document student rights under POPIA with implementation procedures:

1. **Right of access (Section 23)** -- full data export in readable format. What format? How requested? Response timeline (POPIA: within a reasonable time).
2. **Right to correction (Section 24)** -- process for students to request correction of inaccurate data. What data can be corrected? (Self-report responses yes, system-collected events are factual records.)
3. **Right to deletion (Section 24)** -- available AFTER course ends (locked decision: no mid-course deletion). Deletion covers all individual-level data. Aggregated statistics that included the student's data are retained (no longer personal information per research Open Question 4 recommendation).
4. **Right to object (Section 11(3))** -- CRITICAL: document the objection process clearly. Include:
   - How to object (written request to Information Officer)
   - What happens next (processing stops for that individual)
   - Consequences for course participation (analytics are part of the course but objection is a legal right)
   - Flag that legal review is needed for the scenario where objection conflicts with course-integrated analytics (research Open Question 3)
5. **Right to complain** -- right to lodge complaint with the Information Regulator

Include a section on how these rights are communicated to students (plain language, at enrollment, accessible format).
