**Document structure:**

**1. Purpose and Scope**
- Purpose: ensure every metric in the learning analytics specification is checked for bias before inclusion
- Scope: applied prospectively to new metrics only (locked decision: not retroactive auditing)
- When applied: before any metric is added to the specification in Phases 4, 5, or 6
- Who applies it: the analyst/designer specifying the metric (in this project, that means Claude during specification phases, with user review)
- Principle: equity is a design constraint, not an afterthought (research anti-pattern: "equity as afterthought")

**2. South African Context**
Brief section explaining WHY these four specific bias risks matter in the SA higher education context:
- **Load-shedding and connectivity:** Scheduled and unscheduled power outages disrupt internet access. Students in areas with worse infrastructure are disproportionately affected. Mobile data costs force students to ration online activity.
- **Multilingual student population:** Many students are learning in English as a second (or third) language. Academic discourse norms are rooted in particular linguistic traditions. Code-switching is a normal communicative practice, not a deficit.
- **Prior education inequality:** The legacy of unequal schooling means students enter university with vastly different preparedness levels. Metrics that conflate starting point with current effort penalize students from disadvantaged backgrounds.
- **Device limitations:** Many students access the LMS primarily via smartphone on small screens with limited bandwidth. Interaction patterns differ significantly from desktop users.

**3. Per-Metric Equity Review Checklist**

Use the structure from the research document (Pattern 3) but make every checklist item a SPECIFIC, ANSWERABLE question (not vague -- research Pitfall 4). Each item should have:
- The question (yes/no answerable)
- What evidence is needed to answer it
- What a "yes" answer means for the metric (risk identified)

**Section 3a: Connectivity Bias Check**
- Does this metric use time-based measurement that would be distorted by connectivity interruptions? (Evidence: check if the metric formula includes timestamps, duration, or frequency that assumes continuous connectivity)
- Does load-shedding in the student's area affect the data source for this metric? (Evidence: check if the data is collected from always-online systems vs. can be captured offline)
- Is offline activity captured and reconciled when connectivity resumes? (Evidence: check if the metric's data source supports the offline sync pipeline from Phase 3)
- Does the metric penalize students who ration mobile data by limiting online sessions? (Evidence: check if session count or duration is a direct component of the metric)
- Mitigation options if risk identified: normalize for known outage periods, use activity completion rather than time-on-task, weight offline-captured data equally

**Section 3b: Language Bias Check**
- Does this metric use vocabulary richness, lexical complexity, or word count as a component? (Evidence: check formula for linguistic measures)
- Are vocabulary-based measures normed for L2/L3 English speakers? (Evidence: check if norms or baselines account for language background)
- Does academic discourse measurement penalize code-switching? (Evidence: check if mixed-language contributions are counted differently)
- Does the metric assume a specific academic writing tradition that may not match all students' educational backgrounds? (Evidence: check if scoring rubrics are culturally situated)
- Mitigation options if risk identified: use growth-over-time rather than absolute measures, norm within language background groups, count substantive contribution regardless of linguistic form

**Section 3c: Prior Education Bias Check**
- Does this metric conflate prior schooling quality with current learning effort? (Evidence: check if the metric rewards pre-existing knowledge vs. growth)
- Does baseline measurement separate starting point from trajectory? (Evidence: check if the metric uses initial assessment as a baseline and measures change)
- Are thresholds set relative to individual trajectory, not cohort norms? (Evidence: check if "good" is defined as improvement from the student's own baseline)
- Does the metric account for different entry-level digital literacy? (Evidence: check if LMS navigation skill affects the metric independently of learning)
- Mitigation options if risk identified: use gain scores rather than absolute scores, set individual baselines, measure growth trajectories

**Section 3d: Device Limitation Bias Check**
- Does this metric disadvantage smartphone-only users? (Evidence: check if the measured interaction is harder or different on small screens)
- Are interaction patterns normalized for screen size constraints? (Evidence: check if click/tap patterns, scroll depth, or time-to-complete differ by device)
- Does mobile data cost affect willingness to engage with the measured activity? (Evidence: check if the activity requires significant data transfer -- video, large downloads)
- Is the metric measurable on low-bandwidth connections? (Evidence: check if data collection requires always-on connectivity or can work with intermittent sync)
- Mitigation options if risk identified: normalize interaction metrics by device type, avoid penalizing shorter mobile sessions, ensure measured activities are mobile-accessible

**4. Review Decision Framework**

After completing all four bias checks, the reviewer assigns one of four outcomes:

- **APPROVED:** No significant bias risk identified across all four checks. Metric can proceed to specification. Document: "No bias risks identified" with brief rationale.
- **APPROVED WITH MITIGATION:** Bias risk identified in one or more checks, but specific mitigation is documented and feasible. Metric can proceed WITH the mitigation incorporated into its specification. Document: which risk, which mitigation, how it changes the metric definition.
- **FLAGGED FOR REVIEW:** Significant bias risk identified that cannot be mitigated through metric design alone. Requires human review (user/course coordinator) before proceeding. Document: the risk, why it cannot be designed away, what decision is needed.
- **REJECTED:** Metric cannot be fairly applied across the student population. The bias is inherent to the measurement approach. Document: why, and suggest alternative measurement approaches if possible.

**5. Worked Examples**

Include TWO worked examples to demonstrate how the checklist is applied:

**Example A: "Session Duration" metric (likely to surface connectivity and device bias)**
Walk through all four checks. Show that connectivity and device checks flag risks. Show the mitigation (normalize for known outages, don't compare absolute durations across device types). Outcome: APPROVED WITH MITIGATION.

**Example B: "Discussion Forum Posting Frequency" metric (likely to surface language bias)**
Walk through all four checks. Show that language check flags risk (L2 students may post less frequently but with equal substance). Show mitigation (weight contribution quality alongside frequency, measure growth in posting over time). Outcome: APPROVED WITH MITIGATION.

**6. Process and Governance**
- When: equity review is completed as part of metric specification (Phases 4-6). The review is embedded in the specification document for each metric.
- Record keeping: each metric's specification document includes a completed equity review section showing the checklist results and decision.
- Escalation: FLAGGED metrics are escalated to the user for decision before specification proceeds.
- Review updates: if new equity risks are identified during later phases, this framework can be updated and previously-approved metrics re-reviewed.
