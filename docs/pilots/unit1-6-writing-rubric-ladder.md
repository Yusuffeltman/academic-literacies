# Unit 1-6 Writing Rubric Ladder (Weekly Tutor Tool)

Purpose: Track progression to full-essay proficiency by Unit 6 while keeping standards fair across pathways.

Use with:
- [docs/reviewed-module-differentiation-plan.md](./reviewed-module-differentiation-plan.md)

## Outcome Target by Unit 6
All students produce a full essay (700-900 words) with:
- clear thesis,
- 3 coherent body paragraphs,
- counter-argument and rebuttal,
- conclusion,
- at least 3 credible sources,
- accurate citation/referencing,
- revision evidence.

## Performance Scale (applies to each criterion)
- 1 = Emerging
- 2 = Developing
- 3 = Proficient
- 4 = Strong/Independent

## Criteria
1. Thesis and argument focus
2. Structure and paragraph coherence
3. Evidence integration and citation
4. Critical thinking (evaluation, counter-argument)
5. Academic language and register
6. Revision quality (response to feedback)

## Unit-by-Unit Rubric Ladder

## Unit 1 (Diagnostic paragraph, 150-200 words)
Expected floor by end of unit:
- Thesis/focus: states a clear claim in one sentence.
- Structure: one coherent paragraph with logical sentence order.
- Evidence: at least one concrete example (citation optional in diagnostic).
- Criticality: begins to explain why claim matters.
- Language: mostly formal register.
- Revision: can make one improvement after feedback.

## Unit 2 (Two linked paragraphs)
Expected floor by end of unit:
- Thesis/focus: claim maintained across both paragraphs.
- Structure: topic sentence + evidence + explanation in each paragraph.
- Evidence: at least one cited source introduced.
- Criticality: compares two ideas/sources at basic level.
- Language: reduced informal language and clearer transitions.
- Revision: applies feedback to improve organization.

## Unit 3 (Mini-response, 300-400 words)
Expected floor by end of unit:
- Thesis/focus: short intro with explicit position.
- Structure: intro + body + short conclusion.
- Evidence: at least one source integrated and cited.
- Criticality: includes basic evaluation, not only summary.
- Language: maintains academic tone across sections.
- Revision: produces draft 2 with documented changes.

## Unit 4 (Comparative response, 400-500 words)
Expected floor by end of unit:
- Thesis/focus: comparative claim is specific and arguable.
- Structure: two body paragraphs with clear comparative logic.
- Evidence: uses at least two sources with in-text citation.
- Criticality: identifies agreement/disagreement and implications.
- Language: more precise terminology and signposting.
- Revision: improves evidence-commentary balance after feedback.

## Unit 5 (Argument build, 500-650 words)
Expected floor by end of unit:
- Thesis/focus: arguable thesis with clear line of reasoning.
- Structure: intro + two full body paragraphs + closing link.
- Evidence: at least two credible sources integrated effectively.
- Criticality: acknowledges at least one counterpoint.
- Language: consistent formal register and hedging where needed.
- Revision: meaningful structural edits between drafts.

## Unit 6 (Full essay, 700-900 words)
Expected floor by end of unit:
- Thesis/focus: clear thesis and sustained argument.
- Structure: intro + 3 body paragraphs + counter/rebuttal + conclusion.
- Evidence: minimum 3 credible sources, accurately cited.
- Criticality: evaluates competing ideas and defends stance.
- Language: controlled academic voice and cohesion.
- Revision: clear draft-to-final improvement with change log.

## Pathway Support Rules (Fair Differentiation)
Standards are common; scaffolding differs.

Supported pathway:
- Provide paragraph frames and planning templates.
- Require mini-conference before submission.
- Keep same rubric criteria and pass threshold.

Core pathway:
- Use standard prompt and one feedback cycle.
- Encourage independent planning with light supports.

Advanced pathway:
- Add source depth and stronger synthesis expectation.
- Require explicit transfer or extension paragraph.

## Moderation Trigger Points
Escalate to tutor-lecturer moderation when:
- any student scores 1 on 3 or more criteria for 2 consecutive weeks,
- rapid completion is paired with weak criticality/evidence,
- pathway placement appears misaligned with performance trend.
